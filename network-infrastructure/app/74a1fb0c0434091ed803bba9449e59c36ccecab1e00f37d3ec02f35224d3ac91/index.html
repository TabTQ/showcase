A two-line, two-VLAN home network: a fast lane for servers and workstations, a slow lane for cameras and IoT, and a router that moves each lane to the other line if its own line fails. Private remote access is Tailscale; the few public pages leave through outbound tunnels, so no router port is forwarded.

VLAN 10: high speed 1 Gbps

192.168.10.0/24 behind the managed switch. Servers, workstations and the main WiFi 6 access points. Prefers the 1 Gbps fiber.

VLAN 20: IoT and cameras 30 Mbps

192.168.20.0/24 behind a 100 Mbps unmanaged switch. Camera recorder and the floor-2 access point. Prefers the 30 Mbps line.

Failover both ways

If the fiber dies, the fast VLAN uses the slow line. If the slow line dies, the cameras use the fiber. The router does this on its own, with no manual change.

No inbound ports by design. Nothing needs to connect in from the internet: public pages travel on tunnels the servers open outward, and private access rides the encrypted Tailscale mesh.

Why two VLANs

Cameras and IoT gear are chatty and rarely patched. On their own VLAN they cannot see the servers, and their slow link cannot slow the fast one.

Why two lines

A 30 Mbps backup line costs little and turns a fiber outage from "no internet" into "slow internet", for servers and cameras alike.

Why a managed core switch

One managed switch carries the VLAN 10 core. The rest of the house uses cheap unmanaged switches because they only need to pass traffic.

Wake-on-LAN across the network

  1. The Proxmox host's network card stays powered in standby with Wake-on-LAN enabled (re-applied at every boot).
  2. The always-on Pi sends a magic packet (the host's MAC repeated 16 times) as a broadcast on VLAN 10.
  3. The switch floods it to every port on that VLAN; only the sleeping card recognises its own address and powers the machine on.
  4. The Hub then waits for the host to answer and shows its status light turning green.
BIOS matters. The motherboard setting for waking on PCI-E / LAN has to be enabled, or the packet arrives and nothing happens. Wake-on-LAN also only works inside one VLAN, which is one more reason the Pi and the server share VLAN 10.
